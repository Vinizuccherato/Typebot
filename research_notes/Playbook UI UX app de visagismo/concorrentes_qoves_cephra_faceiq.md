# Competitor teardown: Qoves, Cephra, FaceIQ (as of Nov 2026)

> Method note: this research used web search result summaries only. Direct page fetches to qoves.com and appgoblin.info failed (the hosts are blocked by this session's network allowlist), so no product site or App Store page was opened in full. Every claim below comes from search-indexed snippets of the cited URLs. Many third-party sources are **competitor SEO blogs** (softmaxx.io, thefaceanalysis.com, thefacereport.com, realworldappeal.com, glowupface.com, getgloe.app, beautifyapp.me, thefacetapp.com). They are useful for facts they quote from the products, but their judgements are not neutral. Visual-language details (colours, typography, screenshots) could not be verified and are mostly listed as gaps.

## Q1. Identity and disambiguation: which product is each name?

### Takeaway
"Qoves" means QOVES Studio, a YouTube-led facial-aesthetics consultancy that sells a roughly $150/yr human-reviewed membership on the web. Several unrelated iOS apps also use the Qoves name. "Cephra" is a newer analysis product at cephra.app (web, plus Android from SixSeven LLC); it is **not** the migraine-tracker app also called Cephra. "FaceIQ" most likely means **FaceIQ Labs** (faceiqlabs.com), a web subscription platform. At least three unrelated mobile apps also use the "FaceIQ" name.

### Cited Findings
**Qoves**
- Wikipedia names Shafee Hassan and Leo Olsen Guillot as founders. Sources disagree on the founding date (Wikipedia 2024, SoftMaxx 2019, Wheon 2018) and on the HQ (Wikipedia says Delaware and that the channel started in Sydney; SoftMaxx says a London consultancy). The "Qoves Inc." company sits behind the "QOVES Studio" YouTube channel. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoves); [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio); [Wheon](https://wheon.com/shafee-hassan-the-man-behind-qoves-studio/)
- Not every iOS app named "Qoves" is official. App ID 6791538114 ("Qoves: Face Analysis & Glow Up" / "Qoves: Facial Analysis") lists developer APPAI LABS. The same app ID appears on the Qatar storefront as "FaceIQ: Face Coach & Glow Up", which points to a name-squatting or renamed listing. A different Qoves-named iOS app (ID 6786037634, released Jul 2026) lists developer Purestrength Limited. One comparison site says the official QOVES website links to no App Store or Google Play app. — [App Store CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/qoves-face-analysis-glow-up/id6791538114); [App Store QA](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/faceiq-face-coach-glow-up/id6791538114); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6786037634); [beautifyapp.me](https://www.beautifyapp.me/qoves-alternatives)
- A Google Play app titled "Pink Mirror - Facial Harmony" uses the package `com.sixseven.Qoveapp`, and that package is now listed as "Cephra - Facial Analysis". The Qoves naming spills across products here too. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp)

**Cephra**
- Official site: cephra.app. Its metadata says it runs in the browser and states that it is not the migraine tracker called Cephra. — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)
- Name collisions: "Cephra – Private Migraine Tracking" (App Store ID 6746087131, Medical category, 2 ratings, 5.0) is unrelated. So are Cephable and Onyx Ceph. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cephra/id6746087131); [apapps.co](https://apapps.co/cephra.html)
- Android: "Cephra - Facial Analysis" by SixSeven (package `com.sixseven.Qoveapp`, formerly titled "Pink Mirror - Facial Harmony"). — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp)
- No official Cephra face-analysis iOS listing was found. — (search gap; see Gaps)

**FaceIQ**
- FaceIQ Labs (faceiqlabs.com) is a web "self-improvement system for your appearance", operated by FaceIQ Labs, Inc., a Delaware corporation (per SoftMaxx). — [faceiqlabs.com](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/); [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/is-faceiq-legit)
- Other apps that share the name:
  - "FaceIQ - AI Face Rating App" (iOS, developer Vasanth Ramesh, ID 6757316268).
  - "FaceIQ - AI Face Analyzer" (Android, package `co.appnation.faceanalyzer`, publisher 88 Tech, 50K+ downloads).
  - The APPAI LABS "FaceIQ: Face Coach & Glow Up" listing described above.
  - A competitor review explicitly warns readers about "which FaceIQ you mean".
  — [App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/faceiq-ai-face-rating-app/id6757316268); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.appnation.faceanalyzer&hl=en_US); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/faceiqlabs-review)

### Inferences
- In this category, names are crowded and get squatted. Clones capture the brand search traffic of established names (Qoves, FaceIQ) on the App Store. For "facet.", this argues for securing the trademark and App Store name early.
- **Naming conflict to flag:** a product called "Facet" already exists in this exact space. thefacetapp.com publishes a 2026 Qoves review under the brand "Facet". The user's proposed name "facet." may collide with it. — [thefacetapp.com](https://www.thefacetapp.com/blog/does-qoves-work)
- For the rest of this document, "Qoves" = the official QOVES web membership, and "FaceIQ" = FaceIQ Labs. Clone apps are noted only where relevant.

### Gaps
- Whether Cephra has an iOS app (official, or a renamed one) could not be confirmed.
- No confirmed relationship among SixSeven, APPAI LABS, Purestrength and the official brands.

## Q2. Value proposition, audience, positioning, platforms

### Takeaway
Qoves is the premium, "science-flavoured consultancy" anchor: human-reviewed and slow, with a YouTube authority brand, and it edges into surgical recommendations. Cephra and FaceIQ Labs are self-serve, instant, ratio-heavy "harmony score" tools aimed at the looksmaxxing audience. They use pillar-based scoring frameworks, progress tracking and AI chat.

### Cited Findings
**Qoves**
- Distribution flywheel: the YouTube channel QOVES Studio has about 1.05M subscribers (vidIQ, 2026-10-26), up from about 600k in Dec 2022. It is known for celebrity face-analysis videos (for example a viral BTS V analysis). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@qovesstudio/); [Wheon](https://wheon.com/shafee-hassan-the-man-behind-qoves-studio/); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/music/news-most-handsome-man-world-fans-react-qovesstudio-reveals-results-analysis-studying-bts-kim-taehyung-s-facial-features)
- Positioning: a deep, human-reviewed facial analysis with a team of human reviewers and a care-team chat. — [glowupface.com](https://glowupface.com/articles/qoves-review)
- One reviewer explicitly frames Qoves as "not a looksmaxxing app" (Substack review title). — [Uncynical](https://uncynical.substack.com/p/i-tried-qoves-which-isnt-a-looksmaxxing)
- A sample report describes cephalometric measurements compared against "scientific averages" and states it is not a medical diagnosis (older document; date unknown). — [Scribd sample](https://www.scribd.com/document/683724839/Sample-FINAL-Comprehensive-Report-V2)
- Platform: web (qoves.com). The free web "Facial Assessment Tool" has reportedly been removed. — [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Adults only, with self-reported body-image screening. The Guardian noted both checks can be circumvented (as relayed by secondary sources). — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/clavicular-edits-kendall-jenner-face-looksmaxxing-1809122); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)

**Cephra**
- Pitch: two photos become "a precise study of your face", with every ratio drawn on your photo, five pillars, your archetype, a simulation and a plan. Headline figures: "69 ratios · 5 pillars · on your own photo", shown with an example overall score of 8.5. — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)
- Speaks directly to looksmaxxing frustration: "most looksmaxxing advice online contradicts itself and none of it was measured on your face". Disclaimer: automated, geometry-based analysis with general, non-medical suggestions for appearance and grooming. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp); [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)
- Platforms: browser (web app) and Android. The Android listing claims fully on-device analysis, with photos "stored locally and never uploaded". — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp)
- Higher tiers move into medical-aesthetic territory: the Pro tier adds an "after photo with procedures", a plan and "surgeon recommendations for your concern". — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)

**FaceIQ Labs**
- Pitch: "The Self-Improvement System for Your Appearance": 100+ facial metrics across four structural pillars, a community space, and ongoing re-analysis cycles. Looksmaxxing users rely on its ratio-based scoring rather than subjective ratings. — [faceiqlabs.com](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/); [moge.ai](https://moge.ai/product/faceiq-labs)
- The pillars are harmony, angularity, dimorphism and features (per the official plan table). A competitor lists them as Harmony, Angularity, Dimorphism and Health Indicators. — [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans); [thefacereport.com](https://thefacereport.com/guides/faceiqlabs-alternative)
- Celebrity "celebs-wiki" SEO pages (for example "How attractive is X? Face rating 6.9/10") work as a content acquisition engine. — [faceiqlabs.com celebs-wiki](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/celebs-wiki/custom-faceiq)
- Platform: web. Pro includes surgical as well as non-invasive action plans. — [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans)

### Inferences
- All three anchor on "measured, objective geometry" as the antidote to subjective rating apps. That is the category's core trust claim, and none of them has validated it independently.
- Celebrity face analysis is the shared top-of-funnel tactic: Qoves on YouTube, FaceIQ through SEO wiki pages.
- None of the three is a true visagism product focused on hairstyle, glasses, makeup or style recommendations. Their recommendations lean toward grooming, procedures and surgery. That leaves a positioning gap for a beauty and style-led visagism app like "facet.".

### Gaps
- Gender split and age demographics of the audiences were not found. The looksmaxxing framing implies mostly young men, but this is unverified.
- Cephra's team, founding date and location were not found.

## Q3. Business model, pricing, paywall placement, trials

### Takeaway
Qoves has no free tier: a single annual membership of about $150, plus expensive add-ons. Cephra runs a classic freemium ladder (Free → €9.99 → €19.99 → Pro), with a teaser free result that is generous enough to hook. FaceIQ Labs uses a free-teaser plus paywall-after-upload model at $24.99–$49.99/mo. It gets billing-confusion complaints about annual charges.

### Cited Findings
**Qoves**
- One paid membership at $150/yr (seen as €150/yr from Europe in Nov 2026). No free tier or trial. The free part is the YouTube content. — [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review); [thefacetapp.com](https://www.thefacetapp.com/blog/does-qoves-work); [SoftMaxx pricing](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing)
- Add-ons, per the Guardian as relayed: surgical recommendations upgrade $180, express delivery $80. One customer paid $330 for a report that recommended rhinoplasty, eyelid surgery, Botox and lip filler. A Trustpilot reviewer's total came to $255 (membership + express + hairstyle add-on). — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/clavicular-edits-kendall-jenner-face-looksmaxxing-1809122); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- History: older Reddit threads describe one-off reports that have since been retired. A UK Trustpilot reviewer cited about £150. An older directory listed a "Membership Premium Plan" at $150/yr with a money-back guarantee within 4 months (mid-2025). Some sources describe an introductory "QOVES 2.0" pricing structure. — [realworldappeal.com](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-reddit-verdict); [it.trustpilot.com](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [krowdbase](https://www.krowdbase.com/software/facial-assessment-tool); [switchtools](https://www.switchtools.io/hi/tool/qoves)
- Clone iOS app (APPAI LABS): free with Pro subscription, reportedly USD 9.99/week or 49.99/yr. — [App Store BO](https://apps.apple.com/bo/app/qoves-face-analysis-glow-up/id6791538114?l=en-GB)

**Cephra** (cephra.app)
- **Free:** 3 scans every 7 days, a front Harmony score on every scan, and 3 ratios drawn on the photo (strongest, middle, weakest).
- **Basic, €9.99/mo:** every front and side ratio; front, side and combined Harmony; all five pillars and the archetype; 5 scans/month; AI analyst (20 messages/month); simulation for 3 areas per scan.
- **Plus, €19.99/mo:** unlimited scans, full simulator, compare and progress tracking.
- **Pro:** price not found. Adds an after photo with procedures, a plan, and surgeon recommendations.
- — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)

**FaceIQ Labs** (official plans page)
- **Basic:** $24.99/mo, $59.97/quarter or $119.88/yr.
- **Pro:** $49.99/mo, $119.97/quarter or $239.88/yr.
- Prices are in USD, with local currency shown at checkout. — [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans)
- **Free:** landmark mapping, side-profile score, 10 harmony ratios (vs 70+ on paid), and 2 strengths/flaws. **Basic adds:** combined harmony score, the angularity/dimorphism/features pillars, overall score and progress tracking. **Pro adds:** a personalised action plan (non-invasive and surgical), treatment simulations, FaceGPT (AI Q&A about your face) and a higher daily landmark allowance. — [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans); [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/faceiq-pricing)
- Paywall placement: front-face metrics and the improvement plan sit behind a paid plan whose price is shown after upload. — [thefacereport.com](https://thefacereport.com/guides/faceiqlabs-alternative)
- Terms: yearly plans are billed upfront, and all plans auto-renew. — [faceiqlabs.com/terms](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/terms)
- Refunds (per SoftMaxx, not verified against the terms): within 14 days, only if no analysis was generated since payment, first time only. Cancelling stops renewal but does not refund the current period. — [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/faceiq-pricing); [SoftMaxx legit](https://softmaxx.io/blog/is-faceiq-legit)

**FaceIQ clones (for reference)**
- iOS, Vasanth Ramesh: "GoMax Scan" $5.99 and "GoMax Monthly" $7.99. AppBrain's copy shows $75/mo, $65/mo on a 3-month plan, or $57.50/mo yearly. The sources conflict. — [App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/faceiq-ai-face-rating-app/id6757316268); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/faceiq-ai-face-rating-app/ios-6757316268)

### Inferences
- The market's paywall pattern is: let users upload, show one headline score plus a teaser of 2–3 ratios, then gate the full breakdown. Cephra's "strongest / middle / weakest ratio" teaser is a clever, emotionally balanced free result: it shows something positive, not only flaws.
- Upselling to procedures and surgery is the premium tier in all three, which ties revenue to telling users they need fixing. A visagism app can monetise styling (hair, glasses, makeup try-on) instead and avoid that tension.
- Annual-plan billing confusion is a known trust killer (see FaceIQ). Clear price labels on the paywall are a cheap way to stand out.

### Gaps
- Cephra Pro price, trial terms and refund policy were not found.
- Whether Qoves offers any trial or refund in 2026 was not confirmed.

## Q4. User flow: onboarding, questions, photo capture, processing, delivery

### Takeaway
Qoves runs an asynchronous, concierge flow: 6 specified photos, human review, and delivery in up to 28 days. Cephra and FaceIQ Labs are instant flows built on two photos (front and side profile), with landmarks overlaid on the user's own photo.

### Cited Findings
**Qoves**
- Onboarding asks for six specific facial photos, including front, side and quarter-profile views. Results take up to 28 days, or less with the paid express add-on. — [glowupface.com](https://glowupface.com/articles/qoves-review); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Users self-report body-image concerns during onboarding, and the service is restricted to adults. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/clavicular-edits-kendall-jenner-face-looksmaxxing-1809122)
- Trustpilot reviewers report turnaround longer than stated because of demand, though others got their report well before 28 days. — [nz.trustpilot.com](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [it.trustpilot.com](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com)
- Clone iOS app (APPAI LABS): a step-by-step guided scan of 5 quick front-camera photos. — [App Store BO](https://apps.apple.com/bo/app/qoves-face-analysis-glow-up/id6791538114?l=en-GB)

**Cephra**
- Web: two photos, with ratios drawn on the photo and results shown immediately. — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)
- The Android listing describes a single photo, analysed on-device, followed by a personalised daily routine. This differs from the two-photo web flow. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp)
- Free scans are rate-limited to 3 per 7 days. — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)

**FaceIQ Labs**
- Two photos (front and side). Landmark mapping is included free, with a daily landmark allowance that is higher on Pro. — [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/faceiqlabs-review); [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans)
- Trustpilot reviewers praise the onboarding as simple and a strong first impression, and the company replied that it invested in it. Others call the results layout confusing, and the company says it is clarifying the UI. — [ca.trustpilot.com](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/faceiqlabs.com); [nz.trustpilot.com](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/faceiqlabs.com)
- The price appears only after upload (a sunk-cost paywall). — [thefacereport.com](https://thefacereport.com/guides/faceiqlabs-alternative)

### Inferences
- The fast pattern of two photos (front and profile) has become the de facto standard. Qoves' six photos plus a weeks-long wait is the premium, concierge exception.
- Guided capture (step-by-step, front camera) appears even in clone apps. Capture quality also matters a great deal: in the Guardian's Epica example, a skin score moved from 61% to 81% after a retake in different lighting. Strong capture guidance (lighting, pose and distance checks) is a credibility feature, not just polish. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/clavicular-edits-kendall-jenner-face-looksmaxxing-1809122)

### Gaps
- Exact onboarding questions (gender, age, goals, ethnicity) were not found for any of the three. Page fetches were blocked.
- Specific capture overlays, liveness and pose checks, and loading-screen designs were not verified.

## Q5. Results and metrics shown

### Takeaway
All three centre on facial ratios and harmony. Cephra uses 69 ratios in 5 pillars plus an archetype. FaceIQ uses 70+ ratios (marketed as 100+ metrics) in 4 pillars: harmony, angularity, dimorphism and features. Qoves offers cephalometric measurements plus human-written recommendations, from grooming up to surgery. Overall scores sit on a 10-point scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Cephra:**
  - 69 ratios across 5 pillars, drawn on the user's photo.
  - Front, side and combined Harmony scores, plus an "archetype".
  - An overall score shown as 8.5 in the marketing example.
  - An AI analyst chat, a simulation of changed areas, and compare and progress views.
  - Pro: an "after photo" with procedures and surgeon recommendations.
  - The Android version scores eight features: jawline, eyes, cheekbones, nose, lips, symmetry, facial harmony and skin.
  - — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sixseven.Qoveapp)
- **FaceIQ Labs:**
  - Landmark mapping and a side-profile score.
  - 70+ harmony ratios, strengths and flaws.
  - Combined harmony and overall scores.
  - Pillar scores (angularity, dimorphism, features).
  - Progress tracking over time; the homepage example shows 5.1 → 7.42 over 2.5 years, which is marketing.
  - Action plan (non-invasive and surgical), treatment simulations, and FaceGPT.
  - — [faceiqlabs.com/support/plans](https://www.faceiqlabs.com/support/plans); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/faceiqlabs-review)
- **Qoves:**
  - Cephalometric measurements compared with averages.
  - A breadth of ratio and proportion breakdowns (praised on Trustpilot).
  - Recommendations: non-surgical by default, surgical as an upgrade; hairstyle as an add-on.
  - "Preview images" of suggested changes, which some reviewers found unnatural.
  - — [Scribd sample](https://www.scribd.com/document/683724839/Sample-FINAL-Comprehensive-Report-V2); [nz.trustpilot.com](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- One Substack writer reports Qoves' AI was gentle ("told me I'm pretty, but…") while the company still promotes surgery. Another, using a "looksmaxxing algorithm", was told to lose weight. — [Substack](https://spinningtowardssingularity.substack.com/p/an-ai-tool-told-me-im-pretty-but); [Thick Me Up](https://thickmeup.substack.com/p/i-paid-a-looksmaxxing-algorithm-to)

### Inferences
- The shared result vocabulary is: overlay of landmarks and ratio lines on the user's own photo, a /10 overall score, pillar sub-scores, strengths versus weaknesses, an archetype or label, simulation or "after" preview, a plan, and progress tracking. Users of this category now expect these elements.
- None emphasises visagism outputs such as face shape → haircut, frames, brows, beard or makeup as the core. Face shape is notably absent from the official descriptions found.

### Gaps
- What Cephra's 5 pillars are, and its list of archetypes, were not found.
- Whether any of the three shows percentiles or population comparisons was not confirmed.

## Q6. UI patterns, visual language, tone, report format

### Takeaway
There is little verified detail because the sites and stores could not be opened. Confirmed patterns: the user's own photo with drawn ratio lines (Cephra, FaceIQ), a pillar or score dashboard, an AI chat assistant (Cephra "AI analyst", FaceIQ "FaceGPT"), simulations, and progress timelines. Qoves delivers a long-form report from human reviewers plus a care-team chat.

### Cited Findings
- Cephra: "every ratio drawn on your photo". Copy tone is clinical and precise ("a precise study of your face") and includes an explicit non-medical disclaimer. — [cephra.app](https://cephra.app/)
- FaceIQ Labs: reviewers call the onboarding polished but the results layout confusing. — [ca.trustpilot.com](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/faceiqlabs.com)
- Qoves: a historic PDF-style "Comprehensive Report V2" sample circulates on Scribd. The current membership includes care-team chat. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/683724839/Sample-FINAL-Comprehensive-Report-V2); [glowupface.com](https://glowupface.com/articles/qoves-review)

### Inferences
- The category tone is pseudo-clinical, using words like "cephalometric", "ratios", "pillars" and "dimorphism". For "facet.", a warmer, style-oriented tone could stand apart while keeping precise geometry.
- Results-page clarity is a known weak point (FaceIQ), which makes progressive disclosure valuable.

### Gaps
- Colour palettes, typography, data-viz styles, use of 3D/AR, and exact report formats could not be verified. A designer should review the live sites, App Store screenshots, and YouTube walkthroughs directly.

## Q7. Strengths, weaknesses, reviews, controversies

### Takeaway
Qoves has the strongest authority and depth but is criticised for price, slow delivery and upselling surgery. It was the main subject of a critical Guardian feature (5 Aug 2026). FaceIQ Labs gets good marks for onboarding and fast refunds but recurring complaints about billing and annual-charge confusion. Cephra has no independent review footprint yet. All three make scientific claims that experts dispute.

### Cited Findings
- **Guardian feature (5 Aug 2026), as relayed by secondary sources:**
  - It covered paid facial analysis in looksmaxxing culture, mainly Qoves and Epica.
  - Qoves did not respond to questions on methodology.
  - Dr Mitch Brown (University of Arkansas) said the science is "nowhere near clear enough" to sell attractiveness recommendations.
  - — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/clavicular-edits-kendall-jenner-face-looksmaxxing-1809122); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review); [streamlinefeed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/paid-face-scans-sell-science-that-experts-say-cannot-exist)
- **Qoves sentiment:**
  - Trustpilot is mostly positive about thoroughness, with complaints about turnaround.
  - A Reddit consensus (via a blog summary) says the content is more serious than cheap scanners, but the paid analysis can feel generic, is expensive and is slow.
  - Other complaints: unnatural preview images, basic suggestions, and doubts about value.
  - — [nz.trustpilot.com](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [realworldappeal.com](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-reddit-verdict); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- **Qoves clone iOS app (ID 6791538114):** 3.8 from 5 ratings (CA) or 4.2 from 17 (US), so the sample is tiny. A claimed "4.4 from 1,100+ ratings" on a comparison site is unreliable. — [App Store CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/qoves-face-analysis-glow-up/id6791538114); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/qoves-facial-analysis/id6791538114)
- **FaceIQ Labs on Trustpilot:**
  - About 4.1–4.4 from 36–55 reviews on the current pages, versus 3.0 from 18 per SoftMaxx (17 Sep 2026); these are probably different snapshots.
  - Trustpilot notes the company has not invited reviews recently.
  - Praise: fast refunds (around 10 minutes), simple onboarding, and one user with body dysmorphia who found the objective breakdown helpful.
  - Complaints: a charge after cancellation, a "monthly" plan billed $120, a confusing layout, and a 1-star review calling the concept dubious.
